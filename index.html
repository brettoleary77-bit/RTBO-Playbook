Is the customer a mining services contractor? pause clause
If yes, introduce the subscription pause clause (16B) proactively — key commercial differentiator for this segment.
Clause 16B — Subscription pause · Selling tool in W1
Qualifying Event: Customer loses mining services contract when the mine owner terminates their contract.
Notice: Written notice + evidence + site confirmation within 14 days.
Duration: Max 6 months per qualifying event. Once per site per term.
Term impact: Term keeps running — no extension. Does not reset the clock.
Default billing: 30% standby fee invoiced monthly during pause.
Idle asset rate (clause 4.10): Assets recording ≤10 operating hours/month bill at 30% standby fee automatically. Billing doesn't drop to zero — the idle rate applies based on RaptorTech system data.
Resumption: Full fees resume automatically at end of pause.
Exit during pause: Standard termination provisions apply including early termination fee.
How to use it. If the customer is a mining services contractor and raises concern about committing to 24 months when their site tenure is uncertain — introduce the pause clause directly. "If you lose your site contract through no fault of your own, you can pause for up to 6 months. During that period your billing drops to the idle asset rate under clause 4.10 — machines recording under 10 hours a month bill at 30% of the subscription fee rather than the full rate. The term keeps running but your exposure drops substantially." Always describe it accurately: billing doesn't drop to zero, the term keeps running, and exit during a pause triggers the standard termination provisions.

15% rule — check this first, every time (clause 10A)
Calculate the change as a % of the current subscribed units. Within 15% → portal or email request to Customer Success Representative — no formal variation, auto-accepted, billing adjusts from next month. Over 15% → written variation required within 5 business days. Critical: every expansion order carries an independent 18-month minimum subscription period from billing commencement, regardless of the remaining master contract term. Max 25% scope reduction in any 12-month rolling period. Per site, not total agreement.
Example. Site with 40 subscribed machines. 15% = 6 machines. Customer adds 4 → portal/email, no variation. Customer adds 8 → over threshold, written variation required. Those 8 new machines carry their own 18-month minimum term from the day billing starts — even if the master contract only has 6 months left. Customer wants to remove 11 machines (27.5%) → blocked by the 25% rolling cap — Brett's involvement required.
The three expansion paths

Machine expansion — adding machines to an existing site
Check 15% threshold per site first. Portal if within 15%. Variation if over. Same module tier unless upgraded. +$550 or +$700/new machine/month.

Highest-value upsell. +$150/machine/month across the entire existing fleet. No new hardware. Should be pitched within 6 months of go-live. On 50 machines = $7,500/month or $180,000 over 24 months.
